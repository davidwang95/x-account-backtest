# Interpretation and report examples

These examples are synthetic, not real posts or measured forecasts.

Good classification: “At this price I would buy Atlas; cash flow supports the valuation.” → explicit buy, bullish, valuation, with exact evidence and a verified security before scoring.

Good exclusion: “Atlas makes an excellent phone.” → product commentary/no-view unless the source also makes a prospective investment claim.

Good conditional: “If Atlas falls below 50, I would buy.” → conditional, retained but unscored in the unconditional baseline.

Good macro: “Credit conditions will tighten further.” → directional macro view; no retrospectively selected ETF proxy.

Good institutional result: “The primary sample contains 40 scored calls: 21 hits, 18 misses and one neutral. Directional accuracy is 53.8% across 39 non-neutral observations. The sample is too small and dependent to establish a forecasting advantage.”

Bad result: “The AI reveals a powerful 52.5% win rate that proves this account is alpha.” → wrong neutral denominator, unsupported risk-adjusted claim and hype.

Bad timing: moving a missing entry from Monday to Wednesday → changes the measurement and can bias results.

Bad branding: a research-firm logo or Dave Wang author/research byline → misstates automated skill output as authored investment research.

Good attribution: “Dave Wang Automation. AI-generated backtest produced using the X Account Backtest skill. Follow Dave Wang for AI-for-finance content and resources at www.davewang.ai.”
